# Hebrew (he-IL) terms

Form of address: gender-neutral, as Microsoft writes Hebrew for Windows 11. Instructions use "יש ל..." with an
infinitive ("יש לבחור", "יש להזין"); what the reader may do uses "ניתן" or "אפשר"; buttons and menu commands are action
nouns ("התקנה", "ביטול", "סגירה", "עיון"). No gendered imperative, future, or present forms are used for the reader;
"שלך", "לך", and past forms such as "הקלדת" are the same for everyone when written without vowels. What happened is said
in the passive ("הושמט", "לא ניתן היה", "בוטלה"). Progress lines are nouns ("בדיקת החבילה…").

A Hebrew prefix before a Latin word, a number, or a placeholder takes a hyphen: "ב-Windows", "ב-{ $drive }". Quotation
marks are plain "". Digits stay 0 to 9. Reasons that the program inserts after a colon have no full stop, as in English.

Words the program puts into another message (a reason, an error, a start time, a kind of processor, a Windows name, a
select's variant) start with a Hebrew letter or a digit, never a Latin word: the program sets each one off as a unit
that runs the way its first letter does, so one starting "Windows" or "DISM" would be laid out left to right and read
in the wrong order. So "הכלי DISM", "הפקודה winget install", "הסיבה לא צוינה על ידי Windows", "32 סיביות (x86)". No
direction marks are written in the text; the program lays it out.
